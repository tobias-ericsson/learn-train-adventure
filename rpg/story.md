The Midnight Masquerade
Intrigue, romance and danger under the masks.
Summary
The Duke of Virelia’s annual masquerade is the most dazzling event of the year, a whirl of music,
wine, and whispered secrets. But tonight, the glamour is a lie. A powerful faction of nobles, led by
the cunning Contessa, plans to shatter the peace. Their target is the grand chandelier; their goal
is to create a tragedy that will disgrace the Duke and crush his dream of a fairer city.
T o save the Duke and the future of Virelia, you must navigate a labyrinth of lies and hidden
loyalties. Trust is a luxury, and every dance, every whispered conversation, could lead you closer
to the truth or even to ruin. The masks will fall at midnight. Will you unmask the traitors before
they strike?
Adventure Hook
Y our invitation arrived weeks ago, sealed in silver wax and scented faintly of roses. Few beyond
the city’s most esteemed ever step foot into the Duke’s ballroom, and fewer still beneath the stars
of his moonlit terrace. Y ou came for your own reasons: an old friend at court, a mission
whispered by a patron, or simply the allure of the spectacle. But this morning, a different letter
slid beneath your door:
“At midnight, they will strike. The chandelier will fall, the masks will shatter. Find me among the
dancers before the final waltz and I will tell you what others would see ruined in silence. Trust
only the rose upon my mask–and no one else.
”
— Y our Secret Admirer
GM Note
This letter comes alongside this adventure booklet via a handout. Proceed to give this letter to the
player at the start of this adventure.
The goal of the Protagonist is to unmask the conspiracy that will take place tonight. The group
lead by the Contessa will want to stop the Duke’s plan to announce the tax cuts for the populace
and will try to either kill or discredit him in the eyes of the people and nobility. They will have to
do this with the help of their Secret Admirer and their wit and shrewdness.
Playing the Masquerade: 1-on-1 & GM-Alternating Rules
In The Midnight Masquerade, one player wears the mantle of the Game Master (GM), weaving
the world, portraying the guests, and driving the conspiracy. The other is the Protagonist,
stepping into the ballroom to navigate its dangers and charms.
For a unique, shared experience, you may use the GM-Alternating style:
The adventure is divided into four acts. At the conclusion of each act, the players switch
roles.
The player who was the Protagonist becomes the GM for the next act.
This style keeps both players equally engaged and transforms the adventure into a
collaborative story where no one knows all the secrets.
Alternatively, this adventure can be played continuously by a GM and a Protagonist, without
changing roles during its development.

Act 1: The Invitation
Scene Introduction
The goal of this act is to immerse the Protagonist in the opulent yet tense atmosphere of the ball
and provide them with the first concrete clues: the Motive (the Duke's tax) and the Method
(inside help). They will also make their first pivotal contact with the Secret Admirer who will be
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander.
GM NOTE
Due to this adventure’s romantic nature, the Secret Admirer or the Silver-Masked Stranger will
be either a woman or a man. This can be changed to fit the preference of the Protagonist or the
sex of the GM. As a primary love interest in this adventure, it is important to clarify which
character will appear (male or female) before their first appearance at the end of this Act.
Discuss with your player and ask them which one they would prefer in this adventure.

Developments
The Duke stands on a gilded dais, greeting guests with a strained smile. The Contessa, a woman
in a dramatic raven-feathered mask and a gown of deep purple, holds court near the buffet,
surrounded by a clique of fawning nobles. She is the center of a loud, laughing circle, but her

There are four main areas of interest:
The Grand Ballroom: The main dance floor. A perfect place to blend in and overhear
conversations. (map provided with the booklet)
The Buffet Hall: A long table groaning with food and drink. Lord Brixton holds court here, his

Each location will be described in no particular order. When the Protagonist arrives at each
location, proceed with the respective developments.
Grand Ballroom
Read or paraphrase the following text when the Protagonist arrives here:
Y ou are swept into the heart of the masquerade. The polished marble floor is a kaleidoscope of
swirling gowns and polished boots, all moving in time to the soaring waltz. The air hums with a
hundred whispered conversations, laughter ringing like crystal above the music. Directly above,
the grand chandelier hangs like a frozen firework, its light glinting off a thousand facets and
casting dancing reflections across the room.
Developments
This is the social hub. It's the best place to observe the main players and absorb the general
mood of the court. The Protagonist can notice a few things here.
Firstly, they may join the dance in the middle or simply pass through. In order to do so, they will
have to pass a DC 12 Performance Check to dance and/or a DC 12 Dexterity Check to move
through the crowd without hitting anybody. If succeeded, they can overhear a juicy snippet:
"
...heard the Contessa call it 'the Duke's folly' right to his face!" This will allow the players to
notice the Contessa as the central intrigue and perhaps the danger the letter mentioned (and
they will be right!). A failure of this check will make the Protagonist step on a noble’s foot and
draw attention to themselves. This will complicate a check a bit later.
Secondly, they can pass a DC 13 Insight Check while looking at the Duke to reveal that his smiles
are forced and his eyes keep flicking nervously towards the large chandelier. Unbeknownst to the
player, the Duke himself got a similar letter, which roused his suspicions of the Contessa, but he
wants to keep a brave face.
Thirdly, the Protagonist might want to get closer to the Contessa’s group to eavesdrop. However,
this is very difficult without an invitation, as one of Contessa's guards will stop them. They will
have to pass a DC 15 Deception Check to convince them to let them through. This check becomes
a DC 18 if they have brought attention to themselves on the dance floor. If they pass, they will
notice her speaking with some noble women. The Protagonist also notices that she is not
drinking the palace wine, but her own, brought from a private flask, a sign of her paranoia and
distrust.
Lady Serenne
Once the Protagonist wants to leave the Ballroom and go explore other locations, they will be
stopped by a woman draped in sapphire silk, her mask edged in silver filigree. She flits from
group to group, a vortex of gossip.She will introduce herself, flatter the Protagonist saying that
she hasn’t seen them before and that their mask is exquisite.
Lady Serenne wants to hear some information from the Protagonist, but the goal could be
reciprocated. If they pass a DC 12 Charisma Check to flatter Lady Serenne back, she will laugh
and casually flirt with them. If the fail checks, she will keep a straight face, but her tone will
change to a disintered one.
She adds that the Contessa and her retinue are quite in large numbers, perhaps even larger than
the Duke’s guards. That would present an issue if “certain ambitious people” would cause a
scene. But before the Protagonist has a chance to ask her what she meant, she will notice
somebody in the crowd and excuse herself.

Developments
This is a more relaxed, but still politically charged, environment. It's where guests go to talk
business and gossip with their mouths full.
Here, the man of the hour is certainly Lord Brixton who is quite drunk, loudly complaining about

Lord Brixton

Act 2: Whispers in the Garden
In this act, the goal is for the Protagonist to locate Marceline the Poet, secure the ledger of
blackmail, and potentially ally with her. Shifting from the busy ballroom to the garden, the
atmosphere of this chapter will be a little more tense and secretive.
The garden is a network of secluded spots for private conversations. The Protagonist must find
Marceline, but they are not the only ones with business here.
Read or paraphrase the following text when you are ready to begin:

Developments
The Protagonist will be free to explore the Gardens (the map accompanies this booklet for quick

therefore there aren’t many locations that the Protagonist can search. However, they can

The Couple
The Protagonist can hear some voices talking in whispers behind a hedge wall. If they pass a DC
7 Perception Check, they can follow the voices and pinpoint their locations. As they reach them,
they come across a couple sharing some smooches in the shadows. They startle and flee,
leaving behind a half-finished bottle of sparkling wine.
The Captain
As they walk through the Garden pathways, they will lock eyes with Captain Lorne whom they
have seen or heard about. He is talking with some guards but notices the Protagonist. If they
pass a DC 15 Insight Check, they will notice the almost imperceptible shake of his head, which
points them towards the Fountain. If the check fails, they do not notice anything from the
gestures of the captain.
The Quill
By passing a DC 10 Investigation Check on the outskirts of the garden, the Protagonist might
find a solitary bench underneath a crown of roses. On it, they find an elegant, black quill. If they
take it with them to Marceline, she will be grateful and trust them a little more, lowering the
difficulty of the proceeding checks.

Act 3: The Masks Slip
This act represents the climax of the adventure. The Protagonist must now act on their choice at
the end of Act 2 to either confront the saboteur or expose the plot. This act will also include an
interesting mechanic called “Time to Midnight”
.
Time to Midnight
Given the urgency of the situation, we are going to use a new mechanic that adds pressure to the
Protagonist. They have approximately 20 minutes of game time before midnight strikes and the
plot is fulfilled.
In order to keep track of this time, keep a simple countdown from 4 to 0. Each number represents
5 minutes of in-game time. Meaning that at number 4 you have 20 minutes left until midnight.
At number 2 you have 10 minutes left. At 0 the clock strikes midnight.
Certain actions in this act will reduce the number of the countdown, whether they succeed or
not. Therefore this is not just a matter of choice and consequence but of dice as well!
This act will have two paths depending on the Protagonist’s choice at the end of the previous act.
Path A: The Gambit (expose the plot)
Path B: The Intervention (confront the saboteur)
Path A: The Gambit
Read or paraphrase the following text:
Y ou burst back into the dazzling ballroom. The Duke is on his dais, preparing for the midnight

Developments
The Protagonist will have to gain the attention of the crowd and the Duke in order to make their
accusation. However, this will be quite difficult, mainly due to their lack of notoriety and the
festivities that are underway. They can achieve this in multiple ways:
Force through the crowd and stand in the middle, making the accusations. They must pass a
DC 10 Strength Check to push the guests away. If they succeed, they gain the attention of the
people. If they fail, they lose precious time trying to reach the middle. The countdown timer
goes from 4 to 3.
If they allied themselves with Captain Lorne, they would catch his eye in the crowd. He will
drop a table of food and drinks, stopping the music and the people from dancing, allowing the
Protagonist to make his remarks.
They could shout to the Duke, but will draw the ire of the Contessa’s guards which will
clamor around them. If they allied themselves with Captain Lorne, he will dispatch them
with his own guards, allowing the Protagonist to speak. They won’t have to pass any check.
The following section doesn’t really have a format, as the Protagonist will have to make the
accusation: the ledger, the Contessa’s involvement, and the plan to sabotage or injure the Duke.
Have the Protagonist roleplay this section as the guests are listening to them. However, after a
while, the Contessa will step in and will trigger the confrontation with her.
The midnight countdown goes down a number.
The Contessa
First and foremost, the Contessa will slander the
Protagonist, calling them an “upstart” that wishes
to disrupt the festivities and sow distrust between
them. She will demand the Protagonist be
arrested, but will be stopped by the Duke himself
who allows the Protagonist to accuse the Contessa
and her to defend herself. Y ou can find the stats
block of the Contessa at the end of this booklet.
The following section plays out over a series of
contested checks. The Protagonist (the player) and
the Contessa (the DM) will roll in a series of
checks, contesting their rolls. However, this is also
a roleplaying opportunity. Depending on the real-
life arguments of the Protagonist, they might roll
normally, with advantage, or disadvantage.
The checks are as follows:
Round 1: The Duke will ask the Protagonist to show the evidence and convince him of their
validity. This will require a Persuasion Check contested by Contessa's Deception Check.
The midnight countdown goes down a number.
Round 2: The Duke will ask for a witness. They can be:
Captain Lorne (if and only if the Protagonist told him about the plan)
Marceline the Poet
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander will not be present in the ballroom in this path, as she/he is busy
stopping the saboteur from dropping the chandelier.
Captain Lorne will come willingly and attest to the accusations of the Protagonist. Marceline the
Poet will have to be convinced to do this with a DC 15 Persuasion Check. If the check fails, the
Protagonist can call another witness or leave it out. This will hurt in the final decision, but they
still have a way to win.
Round 3: The Duke will ask for some final remarks between the two parties. This is another
contested check. It is a Persuasion Check to the Contessa’s Intimidation Check.
If the check succeeds, the Duke will be convinced of the validity of the Protagonist’s accusations.
If it fails, the Duke is skeptical of their remarks.
The end of the confrontation will have two resolutions:
If the Protagonist wins in two of the three rounds, the Duke will order Contessa's arrest.
Some of the guests, mostly her allies, will shrink away, trying to avoid capture.
If the Protagonist loses two of the three rounds, the Duke is overwhelmed and does not want
to cause a scene. He will tell the guests that this will be resolved after the midnight toast.
The clock will strike midnight at this point. Proceed to the Stroke of Midnight section for the
resolution of this event.
Path B: The Intervention
Read or paraphrase the following text:
The service corridor is a stark contrast to the ballroom's opulence: narrow, dimly lit by a single
swaying lantern, and smelling of dust, metal, and lamp oil. Faint bootprints mark the stone dust
on the floor. From ahead, you hear the distinct sound of metal grinding against metal and a low
curse.
Developments
This is the same corridor that leads away from the Musician’s Gallery possibly examined in Act 1.
However, this time, there won’t be any servant that will stop the Protagonist from proceeding,
thus they can follow the path to the rafters above.
As they approach, they see the man in green-and-gold mask desperately trying to attach a final
charge to the chandelier’s main support chain. They will notice the Protagonist and take out a
hand crossbow from their belt and point it at the Protagonist.
Now, they can either try to persuade him into stopping or to attack him, prompting a combat
encounter. In either case, the support chain of the chandelier must be taken into consideration.
As they approach him, the midnight countdown goes down a number.
The Saboteur
As previously mentioned, the Protagonist can approach this situation peacefully or violently.
Peacefully: the Protagonist might try to talk the saboteur down. This will require a successful DC
14 Persuasion or Deception Check. If failed, the combat encounter will be triggered and another
number will go down from the midnight count. If succeeded, he hesitates, lowering his
crossbow slightly. This could allow the Protagonist to have a first attack and take him by surprise
or make a rush for the support chain.
Violently: the Protagonist either tries to intimidate the saboteur or attack him outright. If they
try to intimidate him, regardless of their roll on an Intimidation Check, he will fire his crossbow
towards them. Have them roll for a DC 12 Dexterity Saving Throw or take 1d6 piercing damage.
The combat encounter is triggered. If they attack, the combat encounter is triggered without any
other repercussions.
Y ou can find the Saboteur’s stats block at the end of this booklet.
Developments
The midnight countdown goes down a number, once the encounter with the Saboteur is over.
However, the job is not done, as the Protagonist must go and disarm the charge set on the
support chain.
In order to do so, they will have to pass a DC 10 Sleight of Hand Check. If they have not killed the
Saboteur and incapacitated him only, he will try to flee. Thus, the Protagonist will have to choose
either to subdue the saboteur or disarm the charge. The following outcomes might occur:
If they kill the saboteur and disarm the charge, the chandelier will not fall.
If they disarm the charge but not kill the saboteur, the chandelier will not fall but the
saboteur will flee.
If they fail to disarm the charge, the chandelier will fall.
Even if the chandelier will fall, the Protagonist still has time to go to the ballroom and evacuate it.
They need only pass a DC 10 Athletics Check to run there. As they arrive, they see Lady
Elara/Lord Eliander and both of them will be able to convince the people to evacuate. If they fail
the check, they don’t have enough time to evacuate everyone.
The clock will strike midnight at this point. Proceed to the Stroke of Midnight section for the
resolution of this event.
Stroke of Midnight
As the clock in the courtyard of the Duke’s
Palace strikes midnight, the resolution of this
eventful night will come to fruition.
Read or paraphrase the following text:
The palace clock begins to strike. The first,
deep, resonant BONG silences the room. All
eyes turn upward. The second BONG... the
third... On the twelfth and final stroke, a
terrible, sharp CRACK echoes through the
ballroom, coming from directly above the
dance floor.
The Climax
The end of this act depends entirely on the Protagonist’s success in their chosen path.
Path A - Ending 1
If the Protagonist wins at least two rounds, the Duke orders the Contessa’s immediate arrest. As
the clock strikes midnight, Lady Elara/Lord Eliander will come down with the saboteur, as they
foiled his plan. The chandelier will not fall and everybody is safe.
Path A - Ending 2
If the Protagonist loses the argument with the Contessa, the Duke will postpone any decision out
of fear of embarrassment. Thus, the chandelier plummets toward the crowd in a terrifying,
beautiful, and deadly rain of crystal and fire. The Duke will be caught underneath and be
seriously injured. The Contessa will not be culpable for anything.
Path B - Ending 1
If the Protagonist stopped or killed the saboteur and disarmed the charge, the chandelier is
secure and will not fall. They will return to the ballroom and offer their evidence. With the help of
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander, they will subdue the Duke into arresting the Contessa.
Path B - Ending 2
If the Protagonist did not stop the saboteur but managed to disarm the charge, the chandelier
will not fall, yet the saboteur will flee. The Contessa’s associate will be loose, leaving her deniable
over everything that has happened tonight.
Path B - Ending 3
Regardless of whether they caught the saboteur or not, if the Protagonist fails to disarm the
charge but succeeds in evacuating the ballroom, the chandelier will fall, but only panic and
minor injuries will be sustained by the guests.
Path B - Ending 4
Regardless of whether they caught the saboteur or not, if the Protagonist fails to disarm the
charge and fails in evacuating the ballroom, the chandelier will fall and will injure many people,
including the Duke.
GM NOTE
Depending on the ending they have achieved, Act 4 will be incredibly short (basically showing the
end of this adventure) or will include an opportunity for the Protagonist to engage in some
romantic events with the NPCs. Take note of the ending your player has achieved and describe it
accordingly in Act 4.
If you are using the alternating GM rule, this is the final switch. The player who was the
Protagonist for Act III now becomes the GM for the final resolution in Act IV. The former GM
takes back the role of the original Protagonist to see the story through to its conclusion.
Act 4: Consequences
The immediate danger has passed, but the night is not over. This act is about resolution,
revelation, and romance. The political fate of Virelia is decided, but the personal fate of the
Protagonist and their chosen companion is just beginning.
GM NOTE
The following events will happen only if the Protagonist did not achieve the Path A - Ending 2 and
Path B - Ending 4. If they have achieved one of these two endings, proceed to the end of this
adventure for their respective resolutions.
There will be three main potential romance options for this adventure:
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander: The easiest choice.
Marceline the Poet: Only if the Saboteur was unmasked (see below).
Captain Lorne: If he was turned into an ally.
The Saboteur Unmasked
If the saboteur was killed or survived (subdued by Lady Elara/Lord Eliander for example) he will
be brought before all the guests and unmasked. He will be unveiled as Lord Wilkin, a minor
noble, and Marceline the Poet’s lover.She will be distraught, and if he is alive, she will renounce
her love for him and depart into the garden. If he is dead, he will curse him for what he tried to
do, and depart into the garden as well.
If the saboteur escapes, the Protagonist and Marceline will never know and Marceline will keep
her love for him, thus voiding any romance with her.
Romance Options & Trust
The following options can be used with all three NPCs, only if they have the choice (see the
requirements above) and if they have achieved any of the endings that allow the ball to continue.
Additionally, their actions throughout the adventure will have influenced their Trust in the
Protagonist. If a certain threshold is not met, the romance will be unobtainable.
In order to romance a character, the Protagonist will have to have 3 points of Trust with that
respective character. At the beginning of the adventure each NPC starts with 0 points of Trust.
Each one has a set of moments that will influence their relationship. They are:
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander
The Protagonist can gain or lose trust in the following ways:
+1 Point - if they have triggered the “A Dance” section in Act 1.
+1 Point - if they have retrieved the ledger in Act 2.
+1 Point - if they chose Path A in Act 3.
+1 Point - if they won the confrontation with the Contessa
-2 Points - if they failed to stop the chandelier fall
Marceline the Poet
The Protagonist can gain or lose trust in the following ways:
+1 Point - if they have triggered the “A Drink to Share” section in Act 2..
+1 Point - if they have returned the quill in Act 2.
+1 Point - if they vowed to save her lover.
-2 Points - if Lord Wilkin is not unmasked as the saboteur (she will remain loyal to him)
-2 Points - if they failed to stop the chandelier fall
Captain Lorne
The Protagonist can gain or lose trust in the following ways:
+1 Point - if they have triggered the “An Understanding” section in Act 2.
+1 Point - if they were honest with him in Act 2.
+1 Point - if they chose Path B in Act 3.
+1 Point - if they successfully subdued the saboteur
-2 Points - if they failed to stop the chandelier fall
Endings
Lady Elara/Lord Eliander will be in the ballroom waiting for a dance. Marceline will be crying in
the garden. Captain Lorne will sit by the Entry Alcove.
If the Protagonist has more than 3 points of Trust and has achieved any of the following endings:
Path A - Ending 1; Path B - Ending 1; they will find their potential lover that will ask them for a
dance (either in the ballroom or under the night sky with Marceline in the garden). They will
express their attraction to the Protagonist.
Read or paraphrase the following text:
Y ou stand on the terrace with your lover, your fingers entwined. Below, the city of Virelia
awakens, its streets buzzing with the news of the Contessa's arrest and your heroism. The Duke
has granted you a title and a place at his side. The future is an unwritten song, and you will
compose it together, the memory of the masquerade a foundation for your new life.
The Unmasking
Only in this ending, the Protagonist and their lover can share an additional romantic moment.
They unmask for the first time tonight and gaze into each other’s eyes, falling into a moment of
passion and love.

they have shared with their potential lover.
